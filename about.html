About This Project

A digital exhibition exploring the textual history of the Ramayana — what the earliest texts say, what later traditions added, and why the distinction matters.

Purpose

Ramayana: Beyond What We Were Told is a digital exhibition that invites readers to explore the textual history of one of the world's greatest epics. Through five investigations, a visual timeline, and a comprehensive source library, this project examines how the Ramayana as we know it today developed over more than two thousand years of composition, transmission, and reinterpretation.

The goal is simple: to help readers distinguish between what the ancient poet Valmiki composed and what later generations added in his name. This is not about ranking one version above another or declaring one tradition "true" and another "false." It is about understanding the layered history of a living text.

What This Project Is Not

We want to be clear about what this project does not intend:

  • This is not an attack on any tradition, community, or faith. The Ramayana is a sacred text for millions. We approach it with respect for its spiritual significance while also engaging with its textual history.
  • This is not a claim that later traditions are "wrong." The multiplicity of Ramayana traditions is one of the epic's greatest strengths. Regional retellings, devotional adaptations, and performance traditions each have their own integrity and value.
  • This is not a political project. The Ramayana has been invoked for many political purposes across the centuries. This project is concerned with textual history, not contemporary politics.
  • This is not a claim of absolute certainty. Scholarly consensus is not unanimity. Dates, attributions, and interpretations remain subjects of debate among specialists. Where uncertainty exists, we acknowledge it.

Our Foundation

This project is grounded in two primary foundations:

Primary Reference

The Baroda Critical Edition

The Critical Edition of the Valmiki Ramayana (1960–1975), produced by the Oriental Institute, Baroda, under G.H. Bhatt and team. This monumental project compared hundreds of manuscripts across India to reconstruct the oldest recoverable text through rigorous stemmatic analysis. It remains the standard scholarly reference.

Source Material

The Supplied Manuscript

The content and arguments presented on this site derive from a supplied manuscript that synthesizes findings from the Baroda Critical Edition, the Princeton translated volumes (Goldman, Pollock), and decades of published scholarship by Brockington, Bulcke, Richman, Ramanujan, and many others. Every claim cites its source.

The Multiplicity of Tradition

One of the most important insights of modern Ramayana scholarship is that there is no single "Ramayana." As A.K. Ramanujan famously observed, there are hundreds of Ramayanas — composed across millennia, in dozens of languages, reflecting different theologies, regional identities, and cultural concerns.

This project respects that multiplicity. When we identify an episode as a later addition to Valmiki's text, we are not saying it should be removed from the tradition. We are saying it has its own history — a history that deserves to be understood on its own terms rather than hidden behind a claim of ancient, single authorship.

The distinction between the original text and later additions is clarification, not demolition. Understanding when and why an episode entered the tradition enriches our appreciation of both the original composition and the later works that built upon it.

Truth deserves inquiry. Tradition deserves understanding.

Our Approach

Each investigation on this site follows the same methodology:

  1. Identify a widely held belief about the Ramayana that has become part of common knowledge.
  2. Trace the belief to its textual source — which text, which section, which manuscript tradition.
  3. Present the manuscript evidence — what the critical edition, linguistic analysis, metrical analysis, and comparative traditions reveal about the episode's history.
  4. Acknowledge what remains debated — where scholars disagree, where evidence is ambiguous, where certainty is not possible.
  5. Let readers draw their own conclusions.

We do not tell readers what to believe. We present the evidence and invite inquiry.

Scholarly Disclaimer

Important Note

The limits of scholarly consensus

This website presents textual and historical arguments based on the cited sources and supplied manuscript. Ramayana traditions are diverse, and some questions of chronology, authorship, and interpretation remain subjects of scholarly debate. The findings presented here represent the current mainstream scholarly consensus, but scholarship is always open to revision when new evidence emerges. Readers are encouraged to explore the cited sources directly and form their own understanding.
