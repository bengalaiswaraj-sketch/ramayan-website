Our Mission: Clearing the Colonial Lies Added to Tarnish Our Gods

Why early British rulers and later interpolations targeted the Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Mahabharata, and Ramayana—and how we are restoring the original truth of Lord Rama and Mata Sita in simple, everyday English.

1. What Is Our Agenda? (In Simple Words)

For thousands of years, India's greatest strength—both in social unity and inner spiritual power—came from its sacred scriptures: the Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, Mahabharata, and Ramayana. Even when foreign invaders attacked our temples and cities for centuries, Indian civilization stood unshaken because ordinary families looked up to Lord Rama and Mata Sita as the highest ideals of truth, love, courage, and righteousness (Dharma).

When early British colonial rulers took control of India in the 1800s, they realized a crucial secret: you cannot mentally enslave a nation as long as its people revere their own scriptures and Gods. So British administrators, missionary scholars, and colonial Indologists launched a systematic campaign to corrupt, mistranslate, and distort Indian scriptures into English—and to amplify late, fake additions—so that Indians themselves would begin to doubt, question, and feel ashamed of their own Gods.

2. How the Colonial Conspiracy Targeted All Indian Scriptures

Many people think English translations of our scriptures were neutral "academic works." History proves the exact opposite. Look at the documented evidence across all major scriptures:

  • The Vedas & Upanishads: Funded by the British East India Company, Friedrich Max Müller wrote privately to his wife in 1866 that he was editing the Rig Veda to "uproot all that has sprung from it." Profound Vedic hymns about One Supreme Reality (Ekam Sat Vipra Bahudha Vadanti) were mistranslated into English as "primitive nature-worship" and "polytheistic babble."
  • The Bhagavad Gita & Mahabharata: Early colonial translators reduced the deep spiritual science of Yoga, Atman, and Dharma into rigid fatalism. Worse, British census commissioners took fluid spiritual qualities (Guna-Karma) and froze them into rigid racial caste categories to divide Indians against each other.
  • The Valmiki Ramayana: Colonial translator Ralph T.H. Griffith (1870–1874) stripped the Ramayana of its cosmic spiritual meaning, translated Ahalya's sacred liberation (pada-sparsha) with the insulting word "spurned," and gave full canonical spotlight to the late 7th book (Uttara Kanda) so that British textbooks could accuse Lord Rama of banishing pregnant Mata Sita and killing Shambuka.
  • The Result We Still Suffer Today: Millions of innocent Indians grew up hearing these distorted stories on TV, in schoolbooks, and on social media—feeling secretly confused about why Lord Rama would act "unfairly," without realizing that Maharishi Valmiki never wrote those incidents at all!

4. Do We Respect Devotional Retellings Like Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas?

Yes, 100%! Saints like Goswami Tulsidas (who wrote the Ramcharitmanas in the 16th century) and Kampan (who wrote the Tamil Iramavataram) were great devotees who saved Hindu faith during dark centuries of invasions. Their poems are treasures of devotion (Bhakti).

However, a smart spiritual seeker must know the difference between poetic devotional symbolism (like the Chhaya Sita shadow motif or the dramatic stage device of Lakshmana Rekha) and Maharishi Valmiki's original historical epic (Adi-Kavya). When someone attacks Lord Rama using a later addition from the Uttara Kanda, knowing the manuscript truth protects your faith and silences false propaganda immediately.
